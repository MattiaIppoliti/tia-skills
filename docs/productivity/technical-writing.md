## What it does

`technical-writing` applies a practical standard for documentation and engineering prose: pick one document mode, then write sentences that carry one unambiguous thought. It does not make every document sound the same; it preserves the document's job and voice.

## When to reach for it

You invoke this by typing `/technical-writing`, and the agent won't reach for it on its own. Reach for it when writing or reviewing an RFC, README, guide, reference, pull-request description, or commit message. For prose that sounds machine-written, use [unslop](https://github.com/MattiaIppoliti/tia-skills/blob/main/docs/productivity/unslop.md).

## Pick the mode

Choose tutorial for learning by doing, how-to for completing a task, reference for facts, and explanation for a bounded why-question. Split material that belongs to another mode rather than mixing instructional hand-holding with a lookup page.

## It's working if

- A reader can say what the document lets them do or understand before reaching the end.
- Every instruction has a clear actor, condition, and action.
- Symbols, paths, commands, and examples match the code that ships.

## Where it fits

`technical-writing` is a reach-for-it-anytime writing standard. It pairs with [writing-for-agents](https://github.com/MattiaIppoliti/tia-skills/blob/main/docs/productivity/writing-for-agents.md) when agents are the reader, and with [unslop](https://github.com/MattiaIppoliti/tia-skills/blob/main/docs/productivity/unslop.md) for a human voice. [ask-mattia](https://github.com/MattiaIppoliti/tia-skills/blob/main/docs/engineering/ask-mattia.md) maps the set.
